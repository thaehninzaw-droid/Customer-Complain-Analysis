# Collections & Credit Reporting Complaints — Banking SOP

**Category:** Collections & Credit reporting
**Covers:** Debt collection calls, credit bureau disputes, inaccurate credit report entries, charge-offs

---

## Inaccurate or Outdated Information on Credit Report

**Complaint type:** Customer says an account, balance, or payment status on their credit report is wrong.

**Common Causes:**
- Payment recorded late due to processing delay
- Account paid in full but still showing a balance
- Accounts belonging to a different person (identity theft or mixed file)
- Derogatory mark older than 7 years (FCRA § 605 retention limit)
- Bankruptcy discharged debt still showing as owed

**Responsible Department:** Credit Reporting / Compliance

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Identify which bureau(s) contain the error: Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion.
2. Pull the bank's own records for the account in question.
3. If the bank's record confirms an error: initiate a Metro 2 correction and notify the bureau within 30 days (FCRA § 623 duties of furnishers).
4. Send the customer a written confirmation that the investigation has begun.
5. If the customer has already filed a bureau dispute: the bureau forwards it to the bank as an e-Oscar notice — complete the investigation within 30 days (45 days if the consumer provides new information).
6. Provide the customer with a free credit report once the correction is made.
7. If the error is due to identity theft: refer to Identity Theft protocol (see below).

## Debt Collection — Harassment or Improper Contact

**Complaint type:** Customer says a debt collector has called them excessively, called at prohibited hours, or used abusive language.

**Legal Framework:** Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) — applies to third-party collectors; some states extend coverage to first-party collection departments.

**Prohibited behaviors:**
- Calling before 8 AM or after 9 PM local customer time
- Calling more than 7 times in a 7-day period (post-2021 CFPB rule)
- Using threatening, obscene, or harassing language
- Contacting the customer at their workplace after being told not to

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Obtain the collector's name, company, date, time, and description of the contact.
2. Look up the debt in question; confirm if it is valid and within the statute of limitations.
3. If the collection is valid but the contact violated the FDCPA: issue a cease-contact letter on the customer's behalf; instruct the collector to communicate only in writing going forward.
4. If the debt is not owed or is time-barred: send a validation letter and dispute to the collector within 30 days of the customer's request.
5. Log the complaint and escalate to Compliance for potential FDCPA violation reporting.
6. Advise the customer they may file a complaint with the CFPB or FTC.

## Charge-Off — Customer Disputes Validity

**Complaint type:** Customer says a charged-off debt does not belong to them, or was already paid.

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Pull the original account records including the charge-off date and balance.
2. If the customer claims identity fraud: initiate the Identity Theft investigation; provide an FTC Identity Theft Report template if they have not already filed.
3. If the customer has proof of payment: review the records, initiate a correction, and update all three bureaus.
4. If the debt is valid and unpaid: confirm the account number and explain the charge-off timeline; offer a settlement if the bank has authority to do so.
5. Issue a written response to the customer within 30 days.

---

## Identity Theft — Fraudulent Accounts on Credit Report

**Complaint type:** Customer finds accounts they did not open on their credit report.

**Responsible Department:** Fraud / Identity Resolution

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Verify the customer's identity thoroughly (do not rely solely on answers they may have obtained from a data breach).
2. Ask the customer to file an FTC Identity Theft Report at identitytheft.gov and provide a copy.
3. Place a security freeze on the customer's accounts at all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) — advise the customer to do the same at the bureau level.
4. Issue a new account number or close and reopen any compromised accounts.
5. Open an Identity Theft dispute for each fraudulent account with the respective bureau within 4 business days of receiving the police report or FTC report (FCRA § 605B).
6. Block the fraudulent trade lines from re-appearing.
7. Follow up with the customer at 30 and 90 days.

---

## Credit Inquiry — Unauthorized Hard Pull

**Complaint type:** Customer did not authorize a hard credit inquiry that appears on their report.

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Identify the creditor that pulled the report.
2. If the inquiry was unauthorized by the customer: draft a dispute letter to the credit bureau.
3. Note: hard inquiries typically have a minimal impact (< 5 points) and fall off after 2 years — explain this to the customer.
4. If the customer suspects fraud, follow Identity Theft protocol above.
5. Advise the customer to place a credit freeze to prevent future unauthorized inquiries.
