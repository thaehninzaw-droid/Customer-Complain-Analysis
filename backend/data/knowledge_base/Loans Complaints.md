# Loans Complaints — Banking SOP

**Category:** Loans
**Covers:** Mortgages, student loans, auto loans, personal loans, payday loans, loan modifications, foreclosure

---

## Mortgage Payment Applied Incorrectly

**Complaint type:** Customer says their mortgage payment was not applied to principal or interest correctly.

**Common Causes:**
- Payment applied to fees before principal/interest (common in delinquent accounts)
- Escrow shortage caused payment reallocation
- Servicer error in payment processing

**Responsible Department:** Mortgage Servicing

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Pull the full payment history for the loan.
2. Explain how payments are applied under the loan agreement (fees → interest → principal is standard when delinquent; principal → interest → escrow when current).
3. If there is a clear servicer error, initiate a correction and provide a written notice within 7 business days (RESPA requirement).
4. If escrow is involved, explain the escrow analysis and how the shortage is being recovered.
5. Send a corrected statement reflecting the proper allocation.

## Loan Modification — Request Denied or No Response

**Complaint type:** Customer applied for a loan modification or forbearance but has not received a decision.

**Common Causes:**
- Application is incomplete — missing income documentation
- Customer does not meet the hardship criteria
- Servicer processing backlog (common during economic distress periods)

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Confirm the modification application was received and log the date.
2. Check the application status in the loss mitigation system.
3. If incomplete, notify the customer in writing of the missing documents within 5 business days (CFPB mortgage servicing rules).
4. If denied, provide the written denial with the specific reasons within 30 days of the complete application (RESPA § 1024.41).
5. Advise the customer of their right to appeal within 14 days of the denial notice.
6. If the customer is at risk of foreclosure while in review, follow Dual-Track rules — no foreclosure sale while a complete loss-mitigation application is pending.

## Foreclosure Initiated While in Loan Modification Review

**Complaint type:** Customer reports they were in active communication about a modification, but foreclosure proceedings were started.

**Responsible Department:** Loss Mitigation / Legal

**Resolution Steps:**
1. **Escalate immediately** to Loss Mitigation Supervisor and Legal.
2. Pull the complete communication history and loss-mitigation portal records.
3. Determine if CFPB Dual-Track Prohibition (Regulation X § 1024.41) was violated.
4. If violation is confirmed: halt foreclosure proceedings; consult Legal.
5. Contact the customer in writing within 1 business day.
6. Document everything — this complaint may result in a regulatory or legal claim.

## Student Loan — Incorrect Balance or Payments Not Applied

**Complaint type:** Customer believes their student loan balance is wrong or payments are missing.

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Pull the payment history and disbursement records.
2. Check if the loan is federally serviced (FSA) or private — resolution paths differ.
3. For federal loans: advise the customer to contact studentaid.gov and their servicer; assist with pulling the National Student Loan Data System (NSLDS) record.
4. For private loans: review the loan agreement and internal records; initiate a payment trace if a specific payment is missing.
5. Send a corrected payoff quote if the balance discrepancy is confirmed.

---

## Payday Loan — Unable to Repay, Requesting Extension

**Complaint type:** Customer cannot make the balloon payment due and wants a rollover or extension.

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Review applicable state laws — many states limit rollover fees or require extended payment plans.
2. Offer an extended payment plan if required by law or bank policy.
3. Clearly disclose any additional fees in writing before processing the extension.
4. If the customer shows signs of financial distress, refer them to a nonprofit credit counseling agency (NFCC member).
5. Do not process a rollover that would violate the Military Lending Act if the customer is active-duty military.

---

## Auto Loan — Early Payoff / Prepayment Penalty Dispute

**Complaint type:** Customer was charged a prepayment penalty they were not told about.

**Resolution Steps:**
1. Pull the original loan agreement and locate the prepayment clause.
2. Confirm whether a penalty is contractually valid.
3. If the customer was not disclosed the prepayment clause at origination (Truth in Lending Act violation): escalate to Compliance for a fee reversal and regulatory reporting assessment.
4. If the penalty is valid and disclosed: explain it clearly and offer a written payoff quote including the penalty.
